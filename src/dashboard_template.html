How long will this take, and how wrong is the obvious answer?

Synthetic data. This docket is generated, not observed — no figure here describes any real court, judge or case. Duration and scale parameters are calibrated against Ministry of Justice Civil Justice Statistics (Q1 2026) and the Commercial Court Annual Report 2023–24; many others are modelling choices. Judges are anonymous identifiers by design.

1 · Drag the observation date backwards

Cases still open have no duration yet, so the obvious approach — average the ones that finished — throws them away. Because the slow cases are the ones still running, that answer is always too optimistic. Move the snapshot earlier and watch the two medians separate.

3 · Summary-judgment grant rates across the bench

Each judge with a 95% Wilson interval. A judge with 150 applications and one with 300 do not warrant equal confidence, so the interval — not the point estimate — is the mark to read. Highlighted judges are those whose interval excludes the panel rate.

Judges are anonymous by design, not by omission. France criminalised the publication of judicial analytics identifying individual judges in 2019 (Article 33 of its justice reform act). The UK and US permit it. Building for the most restrictive plausible jurisdiction costs nothing here and makes the tool portable; identifiers carry the analysis perfectly well.
